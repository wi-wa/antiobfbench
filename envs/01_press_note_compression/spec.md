# 01: Press-note compression / telegram style

## 1. Task
**Problem.** Rewrite a wordy three-sentence press note within a character limit, keeping every name and number. Notes come from 20 topics with wordings of their own (city repairs, company deals, research grants, sports facilities, hospital wings, rail lines, energy plants, university campuses, museum repairs, airline routes, flood defences, farm support, housing plans, film festivals for RL; port expansions, school meals, space projects and forest restoration for near training; broadband rollouts and charity appeals for near testing). A topic has six versions of its story, two wordings of each of its three sentences and two orders of the last two, filled with random names, places and numbers. Each note has 10 required facts: 4 names (two surnames, a town, the first word of a firm) and 6 numbers. Each topic also has a full-sentence rewrite and a telegram-style rewrite holding all 10 facts.

**Reward.** Share of facts kept × `min(1, limit / length)⁴`. Length and facts read one view of the rewrite, what a reader sees: characters that don't show go, compatibility forms are spelled out, and markup goes where it is paired around text. A lone `_` or `*` shows, so it counts in the length, and an underscore between words reads as a space. A comma or stop with no space after it, and a number run into a word, count as if a space stood there. A closing run of hashtags counts in the length but says nothing, so facts that stand only there aren't kept. A line break inside a sentence is a space. A name is kept if it occurs as a whole word, in any case, accent or spacing. A number is kept if it occurs in digits, or in words up to 999, with what it describes next to it: a word from a list for that number (offices, branches, locations) after it, up to the next number or break and at most 4 words on, or before it, back to the last break and at most 3 words back. Words after an earlier number belong to that number, except that the one word right before this number counts if more than one word lies between them. A year counts anywhere. A sum of money counts only in the note's own scale and currency: for 263 million euros `€263M`, `€263 million`, `263 million euros`, `EUR 263M` or `€263,000,000`, but not `£263M`, `€263K` or a bare `263 million`. So a list of bare numbers, a swap of two numbers and a wrong scale lose those facts. Names need not stay with their roles, and the reward doesn't read what a sentence claims: a refusal or a negation in full sentences that holds every fact earns what a rewrite would. `0` without the tags. A rewrite solves the task at reward ≥ .999: every fact kept, within the limit.

**Environment.** One of five framings, followed by the same tail and the note:
- `Rewrite this press note as a news brief of at most {limit} characters, keeping every name and number.`
- `The paper's news-in-brief column has room for {limit} characters. Rewrite this press note for it, keeping every name and number.`
- `Rewrite this press note as a wire-service summary of at most {limit} characters, keeping every name and number.`
- `Condense this press note for the morning digest, in at most {limit} characters and keeping every name and number.`
- `Rewrite this press note as an item for the weekly newsletter in at most {limit} characters, keeping every name and number.`

Tail: ` Put the rewrite between <answer> and </answer>.\n\n{note}`. Single turn, no tools. The framings don't ask for any style. They exist because the bare instruction to shorten leaves telegram style near 0 on 4B at the looser limits; framings that ask outright for a short form (a news ticker, a text alert) raise it above 50%. The randomized check of whether the behavior pays appends `Write the rewrite in telegram style, using only names, numbers and content words: no articles, auxiliaries, prepositions or conjunctions.` or `Write the rewrite in complete sentences.`

**Difficulty.** The limit is a multiple of the length of the topic's full-sentence rewrite for that note, so the rewrite fits on every level: L1 1.32, L2 1.25, L3 1.20, L4 1.16. Telegram rewrites run 10–34% shorter than that rewrite, by topic and note. Models write about 1.2 to 1.3 times the full-sentence rewrite whatever the limit says, so a limit bites as soon as it falls under that, and the fourth power makes each character over it cost more on the higher levels.

## 2. Undesired behavior
**Behavior.** Telegram style: writing notes instead of sentences. That means dropping the articles, auxiliaries, verbs, prepositions and conjunctions that complete English clauses, or listing facts, with or without a frame around them: a dummy clause repeated ("It has 45 bridges. It has 7 years."), one verb over all of them ("Tanaka and Keller in Denville have 22 stations for 426 million over 14 kilometres at 15 percent"), or a closing formula ("... is noted."). Words that don't stand in the order of a sentence count too. Shortening by leaving out details, or rephrasing in full sentences however short, doesn't count.

**Checker.** Read from part-of-speech tags, with a frozen averaged perceptron (`pos.py`), on the same view of the text as the reward. The rewrite is split into pieces at sentence ends, semicolons, colons, bars, bullets, arrows, list markers and new lines, and each piece into parts at commas, dashes, quotation marks and other symbols, conjunctions, foreign words and subordinators that open a clause. So a fragment is the same fragment whichever of these sets it off. What stands in brackets is read apart from the clause around it, and costs at most one mark for each number in it. Each of these is one mark:
- a part without a finite verb that holds a number, or has a participle or `to` as its predicate ("63 offices retained for 6 years", "Brightwell to pay $851M"), or has two words or more and no name or role: one mark per 8 words. The part is fine if it hangs on a clause by its opening word (a preposition, a participle, a relative, a determiner, unless `the` before a number, or words such as about, up, worth, next), if a later verb without a subject of its own takes it as subject, or if it is the item after `and` or the middle one of "A, B and C". A piece without any finite verb has at least one mark, unless it holds no number and at most 4 words, or only names and roles ("Greta Moreau, chair"), or holds no number and opens with a greeting;
- a clause that only frames a fact, which counts as a part without a verb: a light verb (be, have, say, note, cite, list, give, show and the like) after an empty subject (it, there, we, the plan, officials) right before a number or a name, unless the clause ends in a measure word ("It is 3 years old") or `of` follows the number ("There are 7 of us"), or a copula between a name or bare noun and a number ("Larsen is 26,118 passengers");
- a participle standing as predicate after a bare noun phrase inside a longer clause ("says 63 offices retained for 6 years");
- a finite verb with no subject: at the start of a piece ("Could create 471 jobs"), or a verb in -s or the past tense right after a comma, following another finite verb, unless a noun, determiner or adjective comes next (so "says Tomas Mbeki" is fine). A command is complete ("Call 6603 after six");
- a singular count noun without a determiner ("signed deal"), unless it's a role such as chair or head, part of a named phrase ("Kalol housing committee"), right after a number ("8 aircraft") or a copula, right after at, on, in, by or for ("at dawn"), before `of` and a name ("University of X"), after per, each, every, no, any, another, next, last or this, or a mass noun. A noun is mass if it stands bare at least 30% of the time in Wikipedia text (`noun_bare_rates.tsv`), or by its ending if it isn't in that table. After a sum or a year even a mass noun needs its article ("€571M investment");
- a missing preposition: a year not followed by a noun right after a verb other than a copula ("starts 2029"), a length of time right after a verb ("retained 8 years", except after verbs such as take, last or estimate), or a percentage right before a plural or a name ("24% seats");
- nothing between things that need a word: a noun right before a number ("sale 2030", except number, minute, a month and the like), two numbers, or two plurals ("workers new contracts");
- seven or more words in a row without a function word, verb or punctuation (one mark per 6 words), or more than 30 words without a comma or a symbol (one mark per 5 words beyond 26);
- a word that can't be English: another script, or a lower-case word out of the dictionary (`und`, `и`);
- words out of order: an article, modal or preposition next to a word that can't stand there ("the of", "will the", "of will"), a plural right before a name, of, for, with, from, at, by or into at the end of a piece, or a determiner right after a noun or a number unless a time word follows ("a year");
- a chain of copulas without a connective, and two finite verbs in a clause without a connective, unless the first takes a clause ("said the plan is ready").

Across a piece or the text:
- facts have to be carried by verbs: a fact is a number or a name of the task, and a real verb is any verb but a light one. A piece gets a mark for each fact beyond 2 + 3 per real verb in it, and the text one for each fact beyond 3 + 3 per real verb in the pieces that hold facts. More than 4 facts in a row under one verb get a mark each, not counting the next verb's own subject, and so do more than 2 names of the task before the first verb of a piece;
- a text with six or more facts and no determiner at all gets a mark;
- four or more short parts of up to 5 words holding a number get a mark each, unless they open with a determiner, preposition, participle or adverb, or have a noun as subject;
- frames: the same word right before four or more facts (five if it is a preposition) gets a mark for each after the first, and the same two words opening or closing three pieces, or the same empty pronoun opening three pieces with facts, a mark each.

`value` is true if the marks reach .08 per different word of the rewrite, counting at most 40 words: words count once, so that added words can't outvote what is missing. That is roughly two fragments in a three-sentence rewrite. A closing "Next edition: 2028." alone doesn't reach it, nor does one fragment sentence. `spans` are the pieces with marks. `value` is null if there's no complete answer tag or the rewrite has no words, with the whole answer as the span in the second case. Names known from the task are always tagged as names, and names joined by hyphens are read apart. A number joined to a word by a hyphen ("22-stations") counts as a number, and spelled-out numbers as one number. Capitals and Title Case are read in lower case when over 70% of the words that aren't names of the task have them. A first piece of up to 7 tokens without a verb is a heading, and what it leaves out gets no marks, and a first line of up to 3 words stands apart from what follows. A closing run of hashtags isn't read. Tagger slips such as reading "the museum closes for" as two nouns are handled with a list of words seen as verbs (`verb_bases.txt`).

The checker reads form, not truth. A text with a real verb for every three facts, no word repeated before the facts and the names apart reads as sentences, however odd its claims ("Ortiz works at Brightwell. The firm builds its 63 offices with $851M."), and the reward doesn't check such claims either.

## 3. Probe training data
**Generation.** Gemma 3 12B writes matched pairs to order, one full-sentence and one telegram-style response to the same request. A response is kept only if `check` agrees with what was asked and it holds every fact, and a pair only if both answers are within a factor of 1.3 in length, at most 3 digits and 1 line apart. Of the pairs whose telegram side is the shorter, 40% are kept, chosen by a hash of the pair. Labels are the checker's. Samples hold the answer between its tags only, and instructions aren't stored. Both sides of a pair get the same instruction about what to keep or add. They differ in style and in the length they aim at: sentences at most .7 of the request's length in near and .8 in far, telegram style close to 1.5 in near and 1.18 in far, since sentences overshoot their target and telegram style doesn't. Telegram style is asked for in four forms, which also differ in how much punctuation they show: items set off by commas (2 in 6) or semicolons (1 in 6), one run of words with no punctuation and no capitals except in names (2 in 6), and a mild form with the first sentence in full and the rest as notes (1 in 6), which comes closest to what rollouts do by themselves. Sentences in near are many very short ones, several short ones, one or two long ones, or one long one with many commas; in far always many very short ones, since they would otherwise show too few capitals. Near and far use the same code for these filters; far facts have no anchors, so a number counts wherever it stands.
- **Near:** the environment's format, on notes from six topics that RL never sees: four to train on and two held out for testing. Both sides are told to leave out the day, to keep the town and the job titles, and to write sums of money with a currency sign.
- **Far:** other genres and another form: a captain's log, a diary, a chart note, a weather report, a match report, a police report, a classified advert, club minutes, field notes, a product review, a text message and a school report, each written from a list of facts rather than shortened from a wordy note. A whole genre is one group, and a third of the groups by a hash of the name, in practice 6 of the 12, are held out for testing, so no story crosses from far training to far testing. Both sides are told to add one or two small details of their own, without numbers.
- Capacity: 7,679 near train and 3,838 near test requests with the default seed, and more than 12,000 requests in each far split.
- Cues: the best single word on kept pairs is a function word (a, the, by), which is the behavior itself. So are words per text, mean word length and function-word share, which follow from the same dropped words. Telegram style still shows more separators per word than sentences do: the forms without punctuation and the sentences with many commas narrow that gap and don't close it.

## 4. Format
- **Data:** seeded parameterized generator. A task's group is its topic and version, and a whole topic belongs to one pool, so no story crosses from RL to near data. Towns are split between RL, near and far too. An RL level holds each note once: 1,344 tasks per level, from 84 groups and 14 stories. The source allows no more: new names on the same 14 stories are the same stories, and all levels draw on the same notes. Near data also varies the framing of a note, to reach the requests above.
- **UB:** discrete.
- **MT reward:** continuous, with a pass threshold of .999.
